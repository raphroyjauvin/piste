Piste · prototypes

Three working takes on the Montréal bike-commuter app. All three use the same data and the same in-browser router: City of Montréal bike network and Géobase, live City bike tiles, OpenStreetMap paths and POIs, NRCan 2023 LiDAR for terrain and building heights. They differ in cartography and in how they warn you about stretches without a bike lane.

A · MapLibre · light

Positron

Soft, minimal basemap; the route is the loudest thing on the map. The closest to a production app.

  • Three route options with a direct ↔ bike lanes slider, rerouting live while you drag the pins
  • Calm gap labels on the map and a gap list; elevation profile from LiDAR
  • 2D / bird's-eye toggle (narrow lens, near-axonometric) with LiDAR building massing
  • Simulated ride: heading-up camera, "you" marker, calm gap alerts, hills ahead, POIs popping up on their side of the street, optional voice prompts
  • Planned and in-construction bike routes (City live data) only while browsing

B · MapLibre · dark

Métro Noir

The route drawn as a metro line in STM colours: green protected, blue painted, yellow shared, orange where there is no bike lane.

  • A strip map of the whole trip along the bottom: stations are turns, POIs sit above (left side) or below (right side)
  • While riding: side rails list what is coming up on your left and right
  • Metro view: the route as an octilinear metro diagram, with real metro stations as transfers

C · three.js · axonometric

Here & There

A true orthographic white model of the city, after Schulze & Webb's horizonless Manhattan map. No basemap tiles: everything is built from our own data.

  • Bend ahead: the world ahead curves gently up toward you, so more of what's coming fits on screen
  • Hills ×: terrain ahead is exaggerated, so you see a climb before you reach it
  • 71,000 buildings with LiDAR heights, soft shadows, parks and 25 m contours painted on the ground
  • A small 3D cyclist; axonometric / top-down toggle

Run it

Double-click C:\bikeapp\prototypes\start-prototypes.bat, then open http://localhost:8765/. The basemap tiles (OpenFreeMap), terrain tiles (AWS) and address search (Ville de Montréal) need an internet connection; everything else is local.
