Piste

A Montréal bike-commuter app, in the prototype stage. It routes you along bike paths as much as it can, warns you calmly before any stretch without one, and shows the hills before you reach them. It covers the whole island, in English and French, with a winter mode, a wind and rain layer and live BIXI bikes. Built on City of Montréal open data (bike network, Géobase, live bike-path tiles), OpenStreetMap, NRCan 2023 and 2024 LiDAR (terrain and building heights), STM, BIXI, Open-Meteo and Environment Canada radar.

Plan a route

  • Three options with a direct ↔ bike paths slider; the route updates live as you drag the pins
  • Avoid hills and Avoid headwinds sit side by side under the slider; the wind option uses today's forecast to steer routes and suggested rides away from streets where you'd ride into the wind
  • Where there's no bike path, the route line turns a calm orange and dashed, and the panel lists each stretch of 50 m or more
  • A Let's ride! button starts the ride; if it scrolls out of sight, a copy floats on screen (above the sheet on a phone) so it's always one tap away
  • Elevation profile with the climbs shaded, and finer contour lines on the map where the ground climbs
  • Routes across the whole island, worked out on your device in a few milliseconds
  • Planned and in-construction bike routes across the island (City live data) while browsing only

Suggest a ride

  • Pick a start, a loop or a one-way ride, a distance from 1 to 100 km, and an optional stop: coffee, a restaurant, a bakery, ice cream, a bar, a public restroom, a park or viewpoint, a museum or a bike shop
  • Three rides that stay on bike paths, come back a different way, and stop where you asked
  • Optional "Avoid headwinds" (next to "Avoid hills"): it counts the time the wind costs you, and loops head into the wind first

Classic Montréal rides

  • Ten curated rides from 9 to 57 km: Old Port, Plateau and Mile End, Mont Royal, Little Italy and Jean-Talon Market, Olympic Park, Parc Jean-Drapeau, Rivière-des-Prairies, Verdun, the Lachine Canal, and on to Pointe-Claire
  • Swipe through the photo cards; the chosen ride is drawn on the map, with its sights marked by small white circles
  • Routed live on the bike network, so winter mode re-routes them
  • Photos from Wikimedia Commons (free licences), credited on each card

See the terrain

  • Soft elevation colours, LiDAR hill shading and contours every 5 m (bold every 25 m, labelled), with finer 1 m lines where the ground climbs, so a hill shows as denser lines
  • One-button 2D ↔ bird's-eye 3D, with LiDAR building heights and LiDAR terrain
  • All of it covers the whole island, with about 560,000 buildings; the terrain comes from NRCan's 2023 and 2024 LiDAR, which between them cover the island
  • The map layers are tile files, and the browser loads only the tiles for the part of the island you're looking at

Winter mode

  • A snowflake button turns the map icy light blue and routes only on the City's 4-season (winter-cleared) bike network
  • Bike paths that aren't cleared show grey and dotted, and warnings say "without a winter-cleared bike path"
  • Snow and ice status comes from the weather forecast: snowfall in the last 24 hours, the temperature, and a freeze–thaw warning. Real-time snow-clearing data isn't public yet

Wind, rain and live BIXI

  • One button shows Environment Canada radar, drifting wind streaks and a card with the wind, a rain outlook and what the wind does to your ride ("Headwind for 95% of the ride · about +1 min")
  • BIXI stations show the bikes available right now: green, orange when 1–2 are left, grey when empty; tap a station for e-bikes and free docks
  • Forecast from Open-Meteo, BIXI from its public live feed

Ride (simulated)

  • The ride starts at once in bird's-eye 3D, with the camera low and behind you; the terrain swells as a climb comes up, so you feel the hill before you reach it
  • Hill card: "Climb in 260 m, +30 m over 860 m", with the next 800 m of elevation
  • Calm gap alerts, a turn card, and places popping up on their side of the street, steady on the map even in top-down view
  • A sound button cycles chime, voice and off; top-down view on one button

Island-wide, in two languages

  • Routing covers the whole island of Montréal
  • An FR/EN button switches the whole app to Québec French: the interface, turn instructions, map labels and voice. It follows your phone's language to begin with

On a phone

  • The panel is a swipeable sheet: drag the handle up for full screen, or down to a small strip to focus on the map; a tap steps through the sizes
  • Tap a search field and it moves to the bottom of the screen, with the map above it: type an address or tap the map, then ✓ to accept or ✕ to cancel. ✓ on the start moves straight on to the destination
  • There are no zoom buttons on phones, since the sheet covered them: pinch to zoom. The map credits button (the “i”) sits at the top left
  • The phone page shows the app inside iPhone-sized screens (notch, status bar and home bar included), side by side, with a picker for what each one shows
  • On a real iPhone, open this site in Safari and use Share → Add to Home Screen

Run it locally

Double-click C:\bikeapp\prototypes\start-prototypes.bat, then open http://localhost:8765/. The basemap tiles (OpenFreeMap), address search (Ville de Montréal), the weather (Open-Meteo forecast, Environment Canada radar) and live BIXI need an internet connection (so do terrain tiles beyond the island, from AWS, which are only a fallback); everything else, including the relief, contour, building and terrain tiles and the ride photos, is local.
